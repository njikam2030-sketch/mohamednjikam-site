Today, 28 October 2026, is Budget day in Britain. Shortly after Prime Minister's Questions, the Chancellor, John Healey, rose in the Commons to deliver his first Budget, and he wrote it above all for one audience: the people who lend Britain money.

It is also the day I am officially launching moharamadan.com, my notebook on economics, finance, leadership and artificial intelligence, written from London as much as from Africa.

My first analysis reads the Budget through the eyes of the bond market: "The Boring Budget the Bond Market Ordered". Headroom down to £15bn, a 3% bank surcharge limited to 15 months, the 10-year gilt steady at 5.31%, and a quiet decision to end the triple lock from 2030.

On the site you will also find opinion pieces, long reads on African finance, notes, charts, videos and a first research report, "The Price of Time".

I write with curiosity, because the most useful question in economics is still why, and with humility, because markets correct anyone who is too sure of themselves. Disagreement is welcome.
