quote: "The curious task of economics is to demonstrate to men how little they really know about what they imagine they can design."
author: "Friedrich Hayek, The Fatal Conceit, 1988"
order: 9
---
Hayek warns against the planner's pride: the belief that a central authority can know enough to design an economy. Prices aggregate information that no single mind possesses. That warning applies to companies as much as governments. A five-year plan built on precise forecasts of demand, prices and rates is almost certainly wrong. The better approach is to build organisations that learn quickly, keep options open and respond to signals rather than defend the plan.
