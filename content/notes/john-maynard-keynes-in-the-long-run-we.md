Keynes is often quoted as if he were dismissing the future. He was doing the opposite. The sentence attacks economists who promise that markets will "eventually" correct, while people lose jobs, homes and savings in the meantime. Its lesson for policy is that timing is not a detail. A recession that "corrects itself" in seven years has already destroyed a decade of careers. For a finance director, the same logic applies: a business that will be fine "in the long run" can still run out of cash next quarter. Liquidity is the bridge between today and the long run, and the bridge has to hold.
