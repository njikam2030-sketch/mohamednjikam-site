quote: "Economics is a study of mankind in the ordinary business of life."
author: "Alfred Marshall, Principles of Economics, 1890"
order: 7
---
Marshall's definition is a reminder that economics is about people, not equations. Rates, yields and spreads matter because they shape rent, wages, food prices and the decision to start a business. When analysis loses sight of the ordinary business of life, it becomes technically impressive and practically useless. My test for any piece I write is simple: could I explain to a shopkeeper in Douala or a nurse in Manchester why this number matters to them?
