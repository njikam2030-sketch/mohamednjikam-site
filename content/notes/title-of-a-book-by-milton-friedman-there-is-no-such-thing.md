author: "Title of a book by Milton Friedman, 1975"

---
The phrase is older than Friedman, but he made it a principle. Every choice has an opportunity cost, even when the price is hidden. Near-zero interest rates looked like a free lunch for a decade; the bill arrived as inflation, asset bubbles and a painful repricing of debt. The same applies to "free" digital services paid for with data, or to government spending financed by borrowing that future taxpayers repay. The discipline of economics is to ask, every time: who pays, when, and how?
