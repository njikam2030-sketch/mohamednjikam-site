Smith's insight is that markets coordinate millions of self-interested decisions into outcomes that serve others, without anyone planning them. It remains the strongest argument for competition. But Smith also wrote The Theory of Moral Sentiments, and warned against merchants conspiring against the public. Self-interest works for society only inside rules: property rights, contracts, competition law and trust. Where those institutions are weak, as in many emerging markets, self-interest can produce monopoly and capture instead of prosperity.
